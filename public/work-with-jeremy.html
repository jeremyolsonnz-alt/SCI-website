Work with Jeremy

The Guided Imagery Programme is free and self-directed, and it always will be. But practising alone isn't the same as practising alongside others. The Practice Circle is a small group gathering over Zoom, offered on koha — and when something is more personal, we can meet one to one.

"The practices are designed to be used alone. But the first weeks can be the hardest, and knowing there are others working through the same thing — a circle to come back to — can be what helps you keep going."

Everything on this website stays free. The audio, the scripts, the reflection journal, the reading behind it all, none of that changes. What I offer here is my time, and a place to practise together.

Jeremy, developer of the Guided Imagery Programme

The Practice Circle

The Practice Circle is a small group session over Zoom, held regularly, open to anyone using the programme anywhere in the world. Each gathering is an unhurried hour: a chance to be guided through a practice live, with a voice responding to the room rather than a recording, and time afterwards to talk about how the work is going — what's settling in, what's frustrating, what others have found.

There's something the recordings can't give you: the company of other people doing the same quiet work. Cameras can stay on or off. You can speak or simply listen. There's no obligation to share anything, and no one is put on the spot.

What we do together
  • A full guided practice, live and at a gentle pace
  • Settling into a regular rhythm with the core practices
  • Working through the ordinary blocks: impatience, discomfort, the sense that nothing is happening
  • Learning to notice and use the daily rest windows the programme describes
  • Questions, conversation, and hearing how others make the practice their own

The circle is personal support for living well after spinal cord injury. It is not a medical treatment, and it is not a promise of physical recovery — the same honest framing that runs through the whole programme. What it offers is company, experience, and a shared rhythm.

Offered on koha

Koha is a Māori custom of voluntary giving: you give what feels right to you, in response to what you've received. Here in Aotearoa New Zealand it's how a great deal of community life runs. There is no set price for the Practice Circle or for one-to-one sessions, no suggested amount, and no expectation. If giving nothing is what's right for you, come anyway — you are just as welcome, and no one will ever know the difference. Whatever is given helps keep this work going.

When something is more personal

Some of the practices touch on private territory — bladder, bowel, sexual sensation — and some things simply aren't for a group, however kind the group is. For that, we can meet one to one: a quiet hour over Zoom, shaped entirely around you and your body. The same work, the same spirit, just the two of us. These sessions are offered on koha in exactly the same way. You only need to email me.

Before we meet (optional)

If you'd like, there's one small thing you can do beforehand. The site has a short self-reflection called Your Healing Style, eleven questions about how you tend to get through hard days. It takes about five minutes and nothing is recorded. Answering it in your own time, unhurried, tends to give a truer picture than anything we could piece together in the first few minutes of a call.

It isn't a test, and there are no right answers. Whatever comes back is a starting point, not a label. If you're coming to a one-to-one session and you do try it, you can use the copy button on the results and paste what you get into your email to me. If you'd rather not, that's completely fine — we'll simply find your bearings as we go.

About Jeremy

I'm the developer of Living Beyond SCI, a free resource built around three pillars: quality-of-life research, hands-free harmonica, and the Guided Imagery Programme.

I hold a Master of Social Sciences majoring in Psychology from Waikato University, and a Diploma of Solution-Oriented Hypnosis, which I completed with Dr Robert McNeilly at Ampersand in Melbourne in 1998. My graduate research into quality of life after spinal cord injury was carried out under Professor Robert Cummins at Deakin University in Melbourne. The guided imagery approach is something I've been developing and refining for around thirty years.

Dr McNeilly is a medical doctor who studied directly with Milton Erickson, the physician widely regarded as the founder of modern clinical hypnosis. He brought the solution-oriented approach to Australia and taught it with a simple emphasis I've carried ever since: that the person already holds the resources they need, and the work is to help them find their own way to them. That's the spirit the circle is offered in — gentle, respectful, and led by you rather than done to you.

I don't have a spinal cord injury myself. My work has always been alongside the people who do, as a researcher and a guide rather than someone handing down a treatment.
